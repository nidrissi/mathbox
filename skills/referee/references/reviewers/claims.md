# Claim and scope lane

Read the shared protocol and the whole manuscript. Compare promises in the
abstract/introduction/conclusion with exact theorem statements, restrictions,
examples and actual delivered results. Treat proof correctness as a separate
question while making this textual comparison.

- `overclaim`: headline generality or conclusion exceeds its theorem;
- `silent-hypothesis`: a material field, smoothness, dimension, finiteness or
  tameness restriction is absent from the headline;
- `unsupported-framing`: first/optimal/sharp/settles is not supported by the
  paper's examples, lower bounds or verified positioning;
- `inflated-contribution`: one result is counted repeatedly, or a routine
  corollary appears as an unexplained independent achievement;
- `undelivered-promise`: an announced construction, section, application or
  example is not delivered;
- `missing-positioning`: relation to work the manuscript actually cites is
  not explained;
- `thin-contribution`: a specifically identified result is an immediate
  consequence of a cited input, with no explained additional difficulty.

For a mismatch quote the promise in `quote` and the delivered theorem
verbatim in `analysis`, with both locations. Identify the exact quantifier,
hypothesis or conclusion that differs. Normal abstract compression is fair;
file omissions that change an expert reader's assessment of what was achieved.
An unestablished material headline claim may be critical; duplicated
contribution language is usually minor.

Without verified sources, positioning doubts are questions grounded in the
manuscript's own text, not assertions that a result is known or duplicates
another paper. Use `literature-check` for a bounded material source question;
do not search every bibliography entry. A failed search cannot prove novelty.
Assess significance specifically or leave it unassessed: "seems incremental"
is not evidence. Exposition is separate from misleading framing.
